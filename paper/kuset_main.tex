\begin{abstract}
Detecting money-laundering rings within complex transaction multigraphs represents a formidable operational challenge due to structural cycles, flow splitting, and extreme class imbalance. While standard Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) operate predominantly on 1-skeletons (pairwise node-edge connections), higher-order topological deep learning models, such as Simplicial Complex Neural Networks (SCNNs) and Cellular Complex Neural Networks (CCNNs), theoretically capture multi-hop cycle geometry through combinatorial Laplacians ($B_1 B_2 = 0$). In this study, we conduct a pre-registered, reproducible benchmark comparing 1-skeleton GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GINE), higher-order simplicial and cellular models, and a persistent homology-augmented hybrid architecture (\textsc{TopoRingNet}) on candidate cycle classification over the official synthetic IBM AMLworld \texttt{HI-Small} dataset (5,078,345 transactions, 513,991 accounts). Under strict test partition isolation ($V_{\text{test}}$, 0.0\% account overlap), our empirical results demonstrate that topological models and edge-aware architectures effectively identify closed candidate rings, achieving an AUPRC of \toporingnetPrAuc{} ($\pm$\toporingnetPrAucStd{}) compared to \ginePrAuc{} ($\pm$\ginePrAucStd{}) for edge-aware GINE and \gcnPrAuc{} ($\pm$\gcnPrAucStd{}) for standard GCN. We present complete methodology, formal boundary nilpotence audits, and turnkey replication release materials.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Financial fraud and anti-money laundering (AML) surveillance frequently contend with sophisticated structural evasion patterns, most notably circular fund routing or ``smurfing rings'' where illicit proceeds cycle through intermediate accounts to obscure origin. Standard Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) \cite{kipf2016semi}, Graph Attention Networks (GAT) \cite{velickovic2018graph}, and GraphSAGE \cite{hamilton2017inductive}, operate via message passing across 1-skeletons. However, standard 1-skeleton aggregation cannot distinguish true topological boundary voids from collections of independent pairwise transfers without higher-order cellular features.

Topological Deep Learning (TDL) \cite{bodnar2021weisfeiler,bodnar2021cellular} generalizes graph neural networks to higher-order domains, including simplicial complexes (triangular cliques) and cellular complexes (arbitrary $k$-polygons for $k \ge 3$). In parallel, Persistent Homology (PH) \cite{edelsbrunner2000topological,carlsson2009topology} extracts multiscale birth and death filtration intervals ($H_0, H_1$) tracking topological life cycles.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{IBM AMLworld Benchmark \& Bounded Cycle Candidate Extraction}
We utilize the physical IBM AMLworld \texttt{HI-Small} transaction multigraph consisting of 5,078,345 transactions and 513,991 unique accounts. Bounded cycle candidate subgraphs ($k \in \{3,4,5,6\}$) are extracted from transaction logs. A balanced cohort of 200 candidate subgraphs is partitioned into Group-Safe train ($N=140$), validation ($N=32$), and test ($N=28$) splits with verified 0.0\% cross-partition account overlap (\texttt{INV-006}).

\subsection{Topological Formulation \& Boundary Nilpotence}
Let $K = (V, E, C)$ be a 2-dimensional cell complex. The oriented incidence matrices $B_1 \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{|V| \times |E|}$ and $B_2 \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{|E| \times |C|}$ satisfy the fundamental topological identity:
\begin{equation}
B_1 B_2 = 0
\end{equation}
The Hodge 1-Laplacian is defined as:
\begin{equation}
L_1 = B_1^T B_1 + B_2 B_2^T = L_{1,\text{down}} + L_{1,\text{up}}
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Protocol \& Pre-Registered Hypotheses}
All models are trained across 5 random seeds ($S \in \{42, 43, 44, 45, 46\}$) using Adam ($\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, $\text{weight\_decay} = 10^{-4}$) and early stopping on validation AUPRC. The test split ($V_{\text{test}}$, $N = \testSampleSize$) remains cryptographically sealed until final evaluation.

We test three pre-registered hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1:} \textsc{TopoRingNet} improves AUPRC vs. strongest validation-selected GNN baseline (\textsc{GINE}).
    \item \textbf{H2:} \textsc{CellularComplexNet} improves AUPRC vs. \textsc{SimplicialComplexNet} on higher-order cycles ($k \ge 4$).
    \item \textbf{H3:} Adding persistent homology features improves AUPRC over the identical cellular complex backbone.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Our findings demonstrate that both higher-order topological models (SCNN, CCNN, TopoRingNet) and edge-conditioned neural architectures (GINE) achieve strong classification accuracy on candidate rings, substantially outperforming standard 1-skeleton baselines when edge flows are preserved. Because higher-order models achieve ceiling performance on the candidate classification task, comparative $\Delta\text{AUPRC}$ differences remain statistically inconclusive under Holm-Bonferroni error control.

\subsection{Mandatory Benchmark Disclosures}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Multi-Agent Data}: IBM AMLworld \texttt{HI-Small} is a synthetic multi-agent benchmark with simulated laundering rings. No real customer or proprietary banking records were processed.
    \item \textbf{Candidate Classification Scope}: The benchmark evaluates candidate ring classification conditional on bounded cycle extraction ($k \in \{3,4,5,6\}$), not end-to-end full-graph entity alert generation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Code Availability}
We have established a rigorous, open-source benchmark for topological candidate laundering detection. Complete implementation code, trained PyTorch checkpoints, and standalone replication scripts are available in the open release bundle (\texttt{release/replicate.py}).
