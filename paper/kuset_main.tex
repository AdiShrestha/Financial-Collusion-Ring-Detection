\begin{abstract}
We compare tabular, graph-neural, simplicial, and cellular models for pattern-grounded classification of directed transaction cycles in the synthetic IBM AMLworld HI-Small benchmark. The cohort contains \candidateCount{} candidates assigned to \groupCount{} protected account-connected groups. Five-fold outer group cross-validation and three inner group folds prevent account overlap and select decision thresholds; fixed model configurations are repeated over five seeds and predictions are averaged per candidate. Logistic Regression obtains average precision (AP) \lrAP{} (95\% group-bootstrap CI [\lrAPLo{}, \lrAPHi{}]); SCNN obtains \scnnAP{} [\scnnAPLo{}, \scnnAPHi{}], CCNN \ccnnAP{} [\ccnnAPLo{}, \ccnnAPHi{}], and GINE \gineAP{} [\gineAPLo{}, \gineAPHi{}]. In the two-comparison primary family, CCNN exceeds GINE by \rqOneDelta{} AP, but the group-blocked permutation result is not significant after Holm correction ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=\rqOneAdjP{}$). On cycles of length at least four, CCNN and SCNN are nearly indistinguishable ($\Delta\mathrm{AP}=\rqTwoDelta{}$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=\rqTwoAdjP{}$). An exploratory LR--GINE comparison favors LR ($\Delta\mathrm{AP}=\rqThreeDelta{}$, unadjusted $p=\rqThreeRawP{}$). These results support the usefulness of global and higher-order representations on this candidate cohort, but not cellular superiority over the tested alternatives.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} anti-money laundering; cellular complex; graph neural network; transaction cycle; synthetic benchmark

\section{INTRODUCTION}
Circular transaction patterns are a common synthetic anti-money-laundering (AML) typology, but a closed walk is a global structure that ordinary one-hop aggregation does not encode directly. Graph neural networks (GNNs) model pairwise transactions \cite{kipf2016semi,velickovic2018graph,hamilton2017inductive}; simplicial and cellular networks extend message passing to higher-order objects \cite{bodnar2021weisfeiler,bodnar2021cellular}. We ask a deliberately narrow question: how do these representations compare after a cycle candidate has already been defined?

This is a pattern-grounded candidate-classification study, not unconstrained whole-network laundering discovery. Its contributions are an account-grouped comparison of eight model families, an algebraically checked distinction between triangulated simplicial and native polygonal cellular representations, and a corrective statistical analysis whose evidence lineage is explicit.

\section{MATERIALS AND METHODS}
\subsection{Dataset and cohort construction}
IBM AMLworld HI-Small is a synthetic multi-agent financial benchmark \cite{altman2023realistic}. The observed ledger contains 5,078,345 transactions among 515,080 active accounts. Cohort construction is asymmetric by design and must be considered when interpreting accuracy: 40 positive candidates are reconstructed from official \texttt{CYCLE} pattern blocks, whereas 115 negative cycles are extracted from background transactions, screened against pattern accounts, and matched on cycle length and transaction-level covariates. Consequently, the experiment evaluates labels within an already selected candidate population; it does not measure candidate-extraction recall in an unseen network.

\subsection{Representations and models}
For a candidate graph $G=(V,E)$, spatial models receive node and directed edge attributes. GINE incorporates edge features \cite{hu2020strategies}. SCNN represents a $k\ge4$ polygon using a fan triangulation with artificial chords and multiple triangular 2-simplices. CCNN attaches the same boundary as one polygonal 2-cell. With signed incidence operators $B_1$ and $B_2$, every constructed complex is required to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{B}_1 \mathbf{B}_2 = \mathbf{0},
\end{equation}
the boundary-of-boundary identity. Cell features are aggregated from boundary-edge features; neither topological model receives the target label as an input feature.

Eight families are evaluated: Logistic Regression, HistGradientBoosting, GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GINE, SCNN, and CCNN. Model configurations are fixed symmetrically. The three inner group folds select only an F1 decision threshold from a fixed grid; this study does not claim a hyperparameter search.

\subsection{Evaluation and corrective analysis}
All candidates sharing accounts belong to one of 18 protected account-connected groups. Five outer folds partition these groups without account or transaction overlap. Each model is fitted with seeds 42--46, producing \checkpointCount{} checkpoints. For ranking metrics, the five seed predictions are averaged for each candidate before evaluation; seeds are repeated optimizations, not independent statistical samples.

The primary metric is average precision. Model intervals use 10,000 whole-group bootstrap resamples. Directional model-swap permutation tests use 10,000 permutations and the plus-one Monte Carlo correction. Holm correction covers the declared primary family: CCNN versus GINE over the full cohort (RQ1), and CCNN versus SCNN for $k\ge4$ (RQ2). LR versus GINE (RQ3) is exploratory and its $p$-value is not multiplicity-adjusted.

The analysis is a corrective analysis locked after Chunk 18 artifact inconsistencies were found. It is not a prospectively preregistered test: an older registration described different models, a single test partition, Wilcoxon tests, and four hypotheses. We disclose this deviation rather than retroactively rewriting the older document.

\section{DISCUSSION}
The tabular and topological models show strong ranking performance on this selected synthetic cohort, while the ordinary spatial baselines are weaker. This difference should not be read as proof of an intrinsic architectural advantage: global flow summaries may align closely with the matching and official-pattern construction process, and the positive and negative candidates come from different selection paths. The experiment establishes comparative behavior within the cohort, not operational AML effectiveness.

The primary cellular comparisons remain inconclusive. RQ1's positive interval is wide and its corrected permutation test is not significant; RQ2 provides neither superiority nor equivalence. A non-significant result is not evidence that the architectures are equivalent.

\subsection{Limitations}
The data are synthetic, the cohort is small (\candidateCount{} candidates in \groupCount{} protected groups), and group-bootstrap uncertainty is correspondingly broad. Positive candidates are official-pattern reconstructions while negatives are extracted controls. Configurations are fixed rather than extensively tuned, so the comparison does not establish each architecture's best attainable performance. Results may not generalize to natural banking networks, other AML typologies, or unconstrained candidate discovery. The Factory methodology review was a same-model self-adversarial review rather than an independent cross-model or human statistical review. Finally, the corrective plan followed discovery of prior artifact inconsistencies and is not prospective preregistration.

\section{CONCLUSION}
On this pattern-grounded AMLworld cohort, LR, SCNN, and CCNN have high point AP, but the primary tests do not establish CCNN superiority over GINE or SCNN. The most defensible contribution is therefore a reproducible controlled benchmark and a documented negative result for the stronger cellular-superiority claim. The release verifier audits existing artifacts and fails closed on inconsistency; it does not independently recreate the raw dataset from an external source.
